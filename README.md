Real verdicts and settlements establish what this litigation has produced:

- **$55.5 million** — Colorado jury verdict, Crock-Pot Express, December 2024 (reduced to approximately $9.1 million under statutory caps)
- **$9 million** — Indiana jury verdict, Maxi-Matic Elite Bistro, May 2025
- **$27 million** — Settlement involving a child burned over 60 percent of her body, Vasconia cooker, 2015

Typical individual settlement ranges based on reported outcomes:

- Minor burns, clear evidence: $25,000 to $125,000
- Significant burns with medical records and scarring: $125,000 to $600,000
- Catastrophic burns requiring surgery, skin grafts, or causing permanent disfigurement: $600,000 to $2,000,000+

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to have known about a recall before my injury?**
No. If the pressure cooker failed during normal use and caused your burns, that is enough to request a free review. Recall status is confirmed as part of the process.

**What if I threw away the pressure cooker?**
Having the physical product is helpful but not required. Medical records, photos of the injury, a purchase receipt, or recall notifications you received all support a claim. Many cases have been pursued successfully without the original appliance.

**Are pressure cooker injury cases class action lawsuits?**
No. These are individual product liability claims. Each victim's case is reviewed and settled separately. Individual lawsuits typically produce better outcomes for seriously injured people than class action distributions.

**What is the statute of limitations?**
Most states allow product liability claims within two to four years of the injury date. If you are concerned about timing, requesting a case review promptly is important.
